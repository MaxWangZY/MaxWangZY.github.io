# Funding the Next Unit of Compute

Author: Max  
Date: June 6, 2026

## Summary

The AI trade cracked the moment the question shifted from demand for compute to who funds it.

On June 5, 2026, the **Nasdaq Composite fell 4.18%**, its worst single-day percentage loss since April 2025. The S&P 500 fell 2.64%, and the Dow fell 1.35%.

The pressure was concentrated in technology and semiconductors. Nvidia fell 6.2%, Broadcom fell 7.9%, and **Micron fell 13.25%**, making Micron the worst performer in the S&P 500. Micron also erased more than $100 billion in market value during the session.

The price action signaled a structural break in the AI trade. Demand for compute remains strong, but the funding burden has moved to the center of the equity story. **Alphabet is raising $84.75 billion** in equity, Meta is facing reported follow-on equity risk, and Google is leasing outside compute from SpaceX. The system is short of capacity, short of capital, or both, and the market is now repricing who pays.

## What Happened

Five catalysts collided.

- **Broadcom reset expectations.** Fiscal Q2 2026 revenue reached $22.2 billion (+48% YoY), while Q2 AI semiconductor revenue reached $10.8 billion (+143% YoY). Fiscal Q3 guidance called for $29.4 billion of revenue (+84% YoY) and $16.0 billion of AI semiconductor revenue (+200%+ YoY).

- **The market wanted a raise.** Broadcom also reiterated fiscal 2027 AI semiconductor revenue above $100 billion, implying at least +79% growth versus fiscal 2026 AI semiconductor guidance of $56 billion. Confirmation fell short for stocks priced for another upward reset.

- **The jobs report hit duration risk.** Nonfarm payrolls rose by 172,000 in May, roughly double consensus expectations near 80,000 to 85,000. The report pushed yields higher and crushed the market's hope for rate cuts because AI buildouts are immediate-spend, long-duration trades.

- **Big Tech funding became the headline.** Alphabet's $84.75 billion equity raise includes underwritten stock offerings, mandatory convertible preferred depositary shares, a $40 billion at-the-market program, and a $10 billion private placement from Berkshire Hathaway. The transaction changed the debate from AI demand to AI financing.

- **Meta became the next dilution risk.** The reported discussion of a tens-of-billions stock offering moved the issue from one company to a possible Big Tech funding template. Meta fell 5.51% as investors priced the risk.

## Why the Selloff Makes Sense

My view is that the selloff makes sense because the binding constraint has moved from demand to financing. Alphabet is raising equity for AI compute, Meta is the next candidate, and Google is leasing compute from SpaceX. These companies are acting like capacity is scarce, and the market is reacting to the cost of that scarcity.

For a decade, Big Tech returned billions through buybacks. Today, the AI cycle requires raw cash. When dominant platforms slow repurchases and start issuing shares, the market must reprice equity. The business can strengthen while the stock weakens.

## Google and the Compute Shortage

Alphabet is the clearest signal because it is one of the world's most cash-generative businesses, yet it is still raising up to $84.75 billion. AI infrastructure has become too large to fund casually.

The SpaceX compute lease adds a sharper message. **Google will pay SpaceX $920 million per month** from October 2026 through June 2029 for access to about 110,000 NVIDIA GPUs, plus CPUs, memory, and related components. Google already has its own TPU ecosystem, but it still needs outside capacity. The compute bottleneck remains real even for companies with custom silicon.

That supports Nvidia, memory, networking, power, and data center infrastructure over the long run. It also confirms that the bill is getting larger.

## Broadcom's Signal

The Broadcom sell-off was an expectations trap. Broadcom's AI semiconductor numbers were still enormous; investors had simply priced in another upward reset.

Broadcom also raises a deeper sector question. Broadcom remains a custom silicon leader, Nvidia remains the dominant GPU platform, and Marvell has gained attention in AI networking and custom silicon. The market is reassessing the balance between custom ASICs and Nvidia's full-stack GPU ecosystem.

The damage spread well beyond Broadcom. Memory was hit directly, and Micron's decline reflected forced selling across the AI hardware chain rather than a Micron-specific fundamental break.

## Korea Was the Stress Test

Korea exposed the leverage. The KOSPI fell more than 5% on June 5 after the U.S. chip selloff. Semiconductor stocks led the decline, the index closed at 8,161.59, and foreign investors sold roughly 3.5 trillion won of Korean stocks.

The setup was already fragile. The KOSPI had crossed 8,000 for the first time in late May and was up 207% from a year earlier. Margin loans were at record levels, which matters because leverage turns a pullback into a liquidity event.

The iShares MSCI South Korea ETF fell more than 11% intraday, putting it on pace for its worst day since March 2020. Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix dominate that exposure, making the ETF a live stress gauge for memory and the AI supply chain. The AI thesis stayed intact; positioning broke first.

## Market View

The secular thesis remains intact. Alphabet is raising capital, Meta is the next candidate, Google is renting capacity from SpaceX, and Broadcom is still pointing to fiscal 2027 AI semiconductor revenue above $100 billion. Those are expansion signals for compute.

Near-term equity risk has increased. The market now has to digest dilution, higher rates, slower buybacks, and crowded positioning. Every AI-linked stock faces the same question: who pays for the next unit of growth? The answer is increasingly shareholders.

That preserves the long-term thesis while changing the entry point. It also separates real infrastructure winners from stocks that were only riding the theme.

For the next week, the key watchlist is:

1. **Semiconductor stabilization:** whether Friday's forced selling reverses or bleeds into broader tech.
2. **Meta funding risk:** whether the reported equity raise is confirmed, denied, or left hanging over the stock.
3. **SpaceX liquidity drain:** whether IPO demand pulls more cash away from high-beta AI equities.
4. **Treasury yields:** whether the jobs report keeps rate pressure elevated.

AI infrastructure remains one of the strongest secular demand stories in the market. But the trade is no longer free. My takeaway is simple: the next phase of the AI cycle will be judged by the funding plan behind it as much as by demand for compute.

The market has started to ask who pays.
