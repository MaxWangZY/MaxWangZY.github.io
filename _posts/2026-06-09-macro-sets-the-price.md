# Macro Sets the Price

Author: Max  
Date: June 9, 2026

## Summary

The market recovered some ground on Monday, while the main question remained open. After the Nasdaq fell **4.18%** on Friday, June 5, it rebounded **0.9%** on Monday, June 8. The S&P 500 rose **0.3%**, while the Dow fell **0.2%**.

The rebound was concentrated in the same AI and semiconductor stocks that were hit hardest on Friday. The **PHLX Semiconductor Index rose 5.6%**, which showed that investors were still willing to buy the AI infrastructure trade on weakness.

The setup on June 9 is more complicated. The market is now waiting for three things at once: the May CPI report on Wednesday, Kevin Warsh's first FOMC meeting as Fed chair, and the expected SpaceX IPO later this week. The facts point to a market that still accepts the long-term AI demand story. The interpretation is that near-term pricing now depends more on rates, inflation, and liquidity than on the demand story alone.

## What Changed After Friday

Friday's selloff was driven by a combination of macro and sector-specific pressure. The May jobs report was stronger than expected, which pushed investors to price a lower chance of rate cuts and a higher chance of future hikes. At the same time, semiconductor stocks were already extended after a powerful two-month run, so the move turned quickly into a broader unwind across AI-linked names.

Monday's rebound showed that the trade still has buyers. That matters because a market that immediately returns to semiconductors after a sharp drawdown is still signaling belief in AI infrastructure demand. The problem is that valuation and financing risk now sit above the thesis. A strong long-term story can still produce rough short-term prices when rates, dilution, and liquidity all move against it.

By Tuesday, that tension was visible again. AI and chip stocks came back under pressure during the session, even without a major new data release. The market was still digesting Friday's selloff and asking whether the move was only a positioning reset or the start of a broader repricing.

## Why CPI Matters

The May CPI report is the next macro gate. Consensus expects headline CPI to rise **4.2% YoY**, up from **3.8%** in April. Core CPI is expected at **2.9% YoY**, up from **2.8%**.

Those numbers matter because the market has already absorbed a strong jobs report. Nonfarm payrolls rose **172,000** in May, roughly double consensus. In a normal expansion, that would support equities. In the current setup, it makes rate cuts harder to justify while inflation risk is already moving back into focus.

For AI equities, the link is direct. AI infrastructure requires immediate spending on chips, memory, power, networking, construction, and financing. The cash flows are farther out. Higher rates lower the value of those future cash flows and raise the cost of building the capacity needed to generate them. That is why the same economic data can be positive for growth and negative for high-multiple AI stocks.

## Warsh Starts With a Credibility Test

Kevin Warsh's first FOMC meeting is now framed around inflation credibility. The market had been conditioned to expect eventual rate cuts, but that path is harder to defend with inflation still above target, the labor market firm, and energy costs still feeding through the economy.

The base case remains a hold. The risk is that a hold comes with more hawkish language, fewer cut hopes, and a higher probability of future hikes. For equities, the exact policy move matters less than the direction of expectations. If the market starts pricing a longer period of restrictive policy, the most expensive AI names remain vulnerable even if their operating fundamentals stay strong.

## SpaceX Adds a Liquidity Test

The SpaceX IPO is the other major event this week. Current reports point to a roughly **$1.75 trillion** valuation and a limited public float. That combination matters because it can create large early price moves while also drawing attention and capital away from existing high-beta technology stocks.

The index impact may be smaller than the headline valuation suggests. Because only a small fraction of SpaceX shares will trade publicly, its float-adjusted market capitalization will be far lower than the full valuation. S&P Dow Jones Indices has also decided against changing its rules to fast-track newly public megacap companies into the S&P 500. Even so, the offering can still affect market psychology. A large, heavily watched IPO arriving during a volatile AI selloff becomes both a liquidity event and a company event.

The key point is practical: if investors want to buy SpaceX, some of that capital has to come from somewhere. Existing AI winners are the natural funding source.

## The 2022 Parallel

The closest historical reference is 2022, with important limits. In 2022, inflation peaked at **9.1% YoY** in June. The Fed responded with aggressive tightening, including a **75 bp** hike in June, and the Nasdaq Composite finished the year down roughly **33%**.

The current setup is milder. Inflation is far below the 2022 peak, and AI infrastructure demand is much stronger than many of the speculative growth stories that led the prior cycle. Still, the pressure point is similar. When inflation moves higher and the Fed reaction function becomes more restrictive, valuation support for long-duration assets can disappear quickly.

The lesson from 2022 is that rate expectations can overwhelm even strong secular stories for a period of time. The current market can follow that pattern without repeating the same drawdown.

## Market View

The AI infrastructure thesis remains intact. The market continues to want exposure to semiconductors, memory, networking, power, and data center capacity. That demand signal remains visible.

The near-term risk is timing. The sector is crowded, the rally has been sharp, and the macro calendar is unfriendly. CPI, Fed messaging, energy prices, and the SpaceX IPO can all compete for liquidity at the same time.

The next one to three months may be defined by volatility rather than direction. Strong AI demand can coexist with weaker equity prices if the market is forced to reprice rates and liquidity together.

The practical watchlist is:

1. **May CPI:** whether headline inflation meets or exceeds the expected **4.2% YoY** print.
2. **Core inflation:** whether price pressure is spreading beyond energy.
3. **Fed language:** whether Warsh's first meeting pushes the market toward future hikes.
4. **SpaceX IPO liquidity:** whether the offering pulls risk appetite away from existing AI winners.
5. **Semiconductor breadth:** whether rebounds are broad or limited to the largest momentum names.

The market remains committed to AI infrastructure, but it is now questioning the price, the funding path, and the macro backdrop. That combination is enough to keep volatility elevated.
